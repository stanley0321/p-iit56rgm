01ArenaPlus · DigiPlus2026Player 360Gradient boosting · LogReg · RF

Which VIPs are about to go quiet?

The business problem

A handful of high-value players carry a large share of GGR. By the time a VIP manager notices one has stopped betting, the player is usually gone. The team needed a short daily list of who to call next, not another report.

What I built

The churn model behind the Player 360 dashboard. I fixed a cut-off date, trained on the months before it and validated on the most recent months, so the model is always tested on a period it has never seen. Fifteen monthly snapshots and about 30 behavioral features from bets, deposits, withdrawals and active days. I benchmarked it against the rule the CRM team already used, days since the last bet.

What came out

AUC 0.91. The riskiest 10% of players hold 58% of next month's churners. The bigger finding was how homogeneous VIP behavior is. Recency and active days carry almost all of the signal, which is why the simple rule already scores 0.885. The model's value is in ranking and calibration, so the top of the list is the right top.

What it changed
  1. A daily alert of the top 100 at-risk VIPs now sits in the Brand Marketing VIP dashboard, for telesales and campaign follow-up.
  2. The CRM team got evidence that a recency-based trigger is a sound first filter for VIP churn prevention, with the model as the ranking layer on top.

02ArenaPlus · DigiPlus2025–2026CRMHoldouts · Difference-in-differences

Four campaigns a week, measured honestly

The business problem

CRM ran four types of campaign every week: retention, reactivation, churn prevention and cross-sell. Reports compared players who redeemed a bonus with players who did not. That credits the bonus with whatever made those players active in the first place, so every campaign looked profitable.

What I built
  • The weekly target-list engine for all four campaign types, with one generator per day.
  • A 10% holdout on every send, stratified by turnover, GGR, deposits and withdrawals so target and control start out alike.
  • Difference-in-differences for older campaigns that had no holdout.
What came out

For the first time the team could see real campaign efficiency. One slots campaign returned 1.34× its cost, not the 2.98× the old comparison reported. Churn prevention, read month by month, looked like it cost 15.6 points of retention. Read per run against its own holdout, it was a +3.6 point D7 lift, and the same method showed which campaigns returned less than they cost.

What it changed
  1. The holdout became the standard way the team reads every lifecycle campaign.
  2. Offers moved away from players who would have bet anyway and toward high-value segments: VIPs with no recent bets, lapsed VIPs and local high-value players. Promotion cost fell by PHP 10M a year while betting volume (PHP 5.6B) and ARPU (PHP 27) held.

03ArenaPlus · DigiPlus2026SportsbookImplicit ALS · NDCG · Bootstrap

The Philippines bets basketball. The calendar decides which league.

The business problem

Sports promotions are planned one league at a time. CRM wanted to know which players to send a league promotion to, and whether a personalized model could pick them better than the lists they already pulled.

What I built

A league recommender on a year of SABA and Altenar bets from 69K active sportsbook players. Implicit-feedback ALS, written both with a library and as a NumPy solver, against four baselines. Evaluated on held-out October bets with NDCG@10 and bootstrap intervals, and as a promotion-targeting test on the nine biggest leagues.

What came out

Every one of the nine biggest October leagues was basketball. Demand switches on and off with the season. Popularity over a full year (NDCG 0.62) beat ALS (0.40), and popularity from the last 30 days collapsed to 0.31 because the window sat in the NBA off-season. For league promotions, players who had bet that league before hit 74%, against 37% for the model's picks. ALS ranks a player's taste, not their volume, so its lists filled with light bettors.

Calls I made to CRM
  1. Time league promotions to the basketball calendar, such as the NBA and PBA season openers in October, not to last month's traffic.
  2. Target a league promotion at that league's past bettors first. The model does not beat this list.
  3. Use the history + ALS blend where rules cannot reach, such as discovery and the home-page "recommended" slot. It covers 16% of the catalogue against 0.1% for popularity.

04ArenaPlus · DigiPlus2026League Fan DashboardSegmentation · CRM targeting

Every league brings a different kind of player

The business problem

Sports promotions treated every league the same. CRM needed to know who actually bets each league, whether that league brings new players or keeps existing ones, and how much margin it leaves, before choosing the target, the offer and the timing.

What I built

A League Fan Dashboard over 115 leagues on both sportsbooks (SABA and Altenar, Oct 2024 to Oct 2026). For each league it shows demographics, product mix (sports only, mixed with casino), new versus existing players, live versus pre-match, VIP tiers, deposits and retention, plus a daily league brief posted to Lark.

What came out

Leaving the NBA aside, the big leagues split into four clear segments.

  • MPBL brings new players, and abusers with them. 47.5% of its 459K bettors made their first deposit in its window, the most of any big league. It was also the league with the most abuse accounts, and it keeps only 2.3% of turnover as GGR, the lowest of any big league.
  • Overseas leagues are a live, casino-adjacent product. KBL, B.League, Turkey TBL and VTB take 85 to 95% of turnover live. About 9 in 10 of their bettors also play casino, and over 80% come back to bet sports in a later month. Turkey TBL takes 58% of its bets between 10pm and 6am.
  • National-team and fight nights draw event tourists. 65% of FIBA Asia Cup bettors bet no other league, and only 57% come back to sports in a later month. Professional boxing is 94% pre-match with 58% retention.
  • Australia NBL has the most sports-only fans. 18.8% of its bettors never touch casino, the highest outside the NBA.
What it means for CRM
  1. Treat MPBL as an onboarding channel with a gate. Screen its first-time depositors for abuse before any bonus, then route the clean ones into the second-deposit journey.
  2. Run overseas leagues as live cross-sell inventory for casino players, with live alerts at local tip-off times.
  3. Give event bettors a follow-up journey into the next league on the calendar instead of a one-off bonus.
  4. Keep sports-only fans on sports offers, not casino pushes.

05ArenaPlus · DigiPlus2026VIP programSegmentation · Reporting

Tiers that reflect what a player is worth now

The business problem

VIP tiers were set by a player's historic maximum monthly turnover, across all products together. One big month could hold a player in a high tier long after they slowed down. A sports bettor and a casino player with the same turnover landed in the same tier, even though they behave and earn very differently.

What I built

Separate tier ladders for sports and non-sports (casino and slots), each based on monthly average turnover rather than the all-time peak, from V0 to V8.

What it changed
  1. Tiers now track each player's current value by product, so tier movement means something.
  2. The new tiers became the base for VIP analysis and a standard metric in weekly and monthly management reports.

07NSUSLAB · GGPoker2023–2025Poker CRMOptimove · Snowflake · Tableau

Personalized win-back for a global poker room

The business problem

Campaigns used one reward and one template for everyone. Blanket offers paid players who were coming back anyway, and cherry pickers took the reward and left.

What I built

Segments by inactivity, lifetime value, preferred format (tournaments, cash games, Spin & Gold) and region, launched and A/B tested in Optimove. A personalized win-back that reminds players of their own past big win. A daily cherry-picker screen on three rules: value-to-cost ratio below 0.1, weak post-campaign activity and accounts sharing an IP. Reporting moved to Optimove Datashare, Snowflake and Tableau.

What it changed
  1. Inactive players with a past big win came back at a higher rate. They became their own win-back segment, and the pilot returned 3.1× its cost.
  2. Cherry pickers are removed from every target list. Campaign ROI on the GGE brand rose from 2.3 to 3.5.
  3. GGR grew 25% year over year and CRM ROI more than doubled. Automated reporting removed four days of work per cycle.
